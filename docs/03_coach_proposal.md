# 03 — Coach Proposal (~300 words)

**Status:** Draft v1 — send after the verbal conversation, not instead of it. Tighten names once we know the audience (head distance coach vs. full staff).

---

Coach,

Our paper log system works, but it has three problems we all feel: logs get backlogged and we sometimes don't have our sheet until midweek; comments come back a week after the runs they're about; and nothing is visible mid-week — if someone writes "achilles tight" on Tuesday, the staff finds out Sunday.

I want to build us a simple online version of the exact system we already use. Nothing about the coaching changes — same weekly sheet, same distance/time/pace prescriptions, same Sunday hand-in, same comments. Only the delivery changes:

- You enter the week once per training group, adjust individuals as needed, and it's on every athlete's phone Sunday night. No copies, no lost sheets.
- Athletes log each day in under a minute: what they ran, how it felt, notes, questions.
- You get one dashboard: who's logged, every question in one place, and anything flagged — pain, missed days — the day it's written, not five days later.
- Every log is saved permanently: mileage trends, injury history, season comparisons, and real summer logging for athletes away from Grounds.

Cost is effectively zero — roughly $0–25 per month in hosting, which I'll cover during the pilot. No contracts, no per-athlete fees. Commercial tools like Final Surge run about $470 per year and still wouldn't match our sheet or grading workflow.

I'd build it in August and pilot with 8–12 athletes in September, running paper in parallel so nothing breaks. If it doesn't make your week easier, we drop it and we've lost nothing.

What I need from you: thirty minutes walking through one week's sheet so the digital version matches exactly how you write training, and your pick of pilot athletes.

— Will

**Notes on the draft (not part of the send):**
- Leads with the felt pain, not the technology. Never says Supabase/Next.js/PWA to the coach.
- "Nothing about the coaching changes" is the load-bearing sentence — it pre-empts the real objection (threat to coach's system/authority).
- Frames the dashboard as *convenience for the coach*, not accountability of the coach. Do not pitch "we'll be able to see how fast you respond."
- The Final Surge line pre-empts "why not just buy something" cheaply. If the coach wants to evaluate Final Surge first, agree immediately — see 04 §2.
- The parallel-paper pilot + "we drop it and lose nothing" removes all downside from saying yes. The ask is 30 minutes, not a commitment.
